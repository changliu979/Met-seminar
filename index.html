01 · Digitisation of Pluviographs
11:05–11:25
Uisnea O Connor
Digitizing Irish Dines Rainfall Charts using Computer Vision
Tap to read bio & abstract ▾
Bio

My background is in Physics. I have a BSc in Physics with Astronomy from DCU and a MSc in Theoretical Physics from the University of Valencia. I'm now a PhD student at UCD in statistics/AI for weather and climate with the AIMSIR group. I worked on an internship for Met Éireann between August and November of this year on a project supervised by Darshana.

Abstract

During my 12-week internship with Met Éireann, I developed a computer-vision algorithm to automate the digitisation of historical Irish Dines pluviograph charts. These charts contain valuable historical rainfall records, but extracting the data has traditionally been a labour-intensive process carried out manually.

The algorithm takes a scanned pluviograph chart and processes it in three main stages. First, image-processing techniques are used to identify the gridlines on the chart, allowing the relevant graph-paper region of interest (ROI) to be isolated and providing a reference for converting image pixels into rainfall and time measurements. Second, the rainfall trace is separated from the background and extracted using a dynamic-programming path-finding algorithm, which follows the trace across the chart. Finally, the extracted pixel coordinates are converted into physical rainfall and time data using the detected gridlines, allowing the rainfall record to be reconstructed digitally.

The algorithm was evaluated on over 100 charts through visual inspection of the extracted traces overlaid on the original scans, alongside comparisons with cumulative rainfall graphs from corresponding digital observations. Approximately 60% of charts were successfully digitised without any manual adjustments, increasing to around 85% with minor adjustments to the algorithm's parameters.

This work demonstrates the potential for computer-vision techniques to significantly reduce the time and effort required to digitise historical rainfall records, making these valuable observations more accessible for future meteorological and climatological analysis.

02 · Rainfall Quality Control
11:25–11:45
Róisín Duggan
Exploring the Quality of Volunteer Rainfall Observations in Ireland
Tap to read bio & abstract ▾
Bio

Roisin Duggan graduated from the University of Galway in October 2026 with a BSc in Geography and Geosystems. She has a strong interest in climate science, hydrology and environmental data analysis, with previous research experience in palaeoclimatology. During her internship at Met Éireann, she investigated the quality of volunteer rainfall observations, focusing on missing reports, accumulated rainfall and their implications for rainfall quality control.

Abstract

Volunteer rainfall observations play an important role in Ireland's climate monitoring network, but missing reports and accumulated rainfall can introduce uncertainty into daily rainfall records. This project explores the quality of volunteer rainfall observations using Met Éireann's provisional and final quality-controlled rainfall datasets, together with 1 km gridded rainfall data from 2025. The analysis examines how missing observations, rainfall conditions and reporting patterns influence quality control adjustments. Results show that missing reports on preceding days are associated with larger rainfall corrections, highlighting the importance of identifying delayed and accumulated rainfall. The findings provide useful insights for future quality control of volunteer rainfall observations at Met Éireann.

Optimising Rainfall Quality Control and Radar–Gauge Integration Using High-Performance Computing
Tap to read bio & abstract ▾
Bio

Yueer Chen recently completed an MSc in High-Performance Computing at Trinity College Dublin. His MSc project, carried out in collaboration with Met Éireann, focused on optimizing an existing rainfall quality control workflow using high-performance computing techniques. His work significantly improved computational efficiency while preserving the scientific integrity of the original workflow. The resulting software has strong potential for integration into operational climate data processing systems.

Abstract

Reliable rainfall observations are essential for climate monitoring and meteorological services. This project focuses on optimising Met Éireann’s existing rainfall quality control workflow, which integrates rain gauge observations and radar data. Different high-performance computing (HPC) techniques were applied to improve computational efficiency while preserving the underlying scientific methodology. The optimised workflow was validated using rainfall data from 2025, demonstrating substantial reductions in processing time while maintaining strong agreement with the original results. This work provides a foundation for future operational implementation.

Marc Lane
Chained Equations for Incomplete Climate Networks
Tap to read bio & abstract ▾
Bio

Marc Lane holds a degree in Physics and Astrophysics from Trinity College Dublin and has recently completed an MSc in High Performance Computing at Trinity. He carried out his MSc thesis project with Met Éireann from May to August 2026, supervised by Dr Brian O’Sullivan. He now works as an HPC Engineer in the Computational Earth Sciences Group at the Barcelona Supercomputing Center.

Abstract

Met Éireann collects daily rainfall data across a large network of stations throughout the Republic of Ireland. These records contain large numbers of missing values due to factors such as stations opening and closing or equipment malfunctions. O’Sullivan and Kelly introduced two new methods based on chained equations for imputing rainfall data from Met Éireann’s network: elastic-net chained equations (ENCE) and multiple imputation by chained equations with direct use of regularised regression (MICE DURR). This project applies them to ten years of daily rainfall data across 523 stations, where the much larger volume of data presents a high performance computing problem. It also introduces synchronous updates, a novel modification to the structure of chained equations that removes a serial dependency and allows the algorithm to be run in parallel. When implemented in C with OpenMP for shared memory parallelism, synchronous updates gave speedups of 8.62x and 5.71x over the original serial R versions. Both methods consistently outperformed other well-known imputation methods in terms of accuracy across four validation metrics.
